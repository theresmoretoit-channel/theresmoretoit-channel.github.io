Why Is Vegetable Basically Just Tomatoes and Cucumbers?

10 min2026-09-04

Researchers took three hundred and ninety-eight tomato varieties, analysed what is in them and had people taste a selection. Modern commercial varieties really do contain significantly less of the compounds that make a tomato taste of tomato.

And one of those losses was not a trade-off at all. It was an accident: growers wanted fruit that ripens evenly, so breeders selected a variety without the dark green shoulder around the stem. That trait turned out to be one gene — and the same gene builds the chloroplasts inside the fruit. An unripe tomato quietly photosynthesises and makes part of its own sugar. The full narration of the video.

0:00Intro

Have you ever noticed something strange about vegetables? Whenever someone says: "You should eat more vegetables." What do they actually mean? Tomatoes. Cucumber. And, in the better case… a pepper. That's it. Apparently the entire plant kingdom has been reduced to three things you can slice into thin pieces and put next to a schnitzel. And I'm getting a little tired of it. Especially the tomatoes. Because unless you have a really good one, picked when it is actually ripe… a supermarket tomato can taste like someone described a tomato to water.

And the cucumber isn't much better. Crunchy? Yes. Refreshing? Sure. Does it actually taste of anything? Well… sometimes. So why do we eat these things all the time? And more importantly… is there actually any other vegetable? Spoiler: there are quite a few.

1:01Somebody actually measured it

But before we get to those… that thing about the tomato. It is not just a feeling. Somebody went and measured it. Researchers in Florida and China took three hundred and ninety-eight different tomato varieties and analysed what is actually in them. Then they sat panels of people down to taste a selection of them, to find out which of those chemicals we actually notice. The answer was not subtle. Modern commercial varieties have significantly less of the compounds that make a tomato taste of tomato.

1:38Why these two are everywhere

So let's back up. Why are tomatoes and cucumbers everywhere? They are incredibly convenient. You don't have to cook them. You don't even really have to prepare them. Wash. Slice. Put on plate. Congratulations. You have vegetables. They also work with almost everything. Meat? Cucumber and tomato. Sandwich? Cucumber and tomato. Burger? Tomato. Salad? Tomato and cucumber. Breakfast? You guessed it. And if you're lucky… someone brings a pepper. It's basically the default vegetable starter pack. But there is one problem. The vegetables that are easiest to eat aren't necessarily the vegetables with the most interesting flavours.

2:21What a good tomato can be

A tomato can be absolutely fantastic. A really ripe one, grown well, can be sweet, acidic, aromatic and incredibly flavourful. But that is not necessarily what we get in a supermarket.

2:35What a supermarket tomato has to survive

A commercial tomato has to survive harvesting, packaging, transport, storage, and sitting on a shelf without turning into tomato soup. So there are good reasons to choose varieties that are firm and durable. And sometimes that comes at the expense of flavour.

2:54The loss nobody ordered

Some of that is the price of durability. But one of the losses was not a trade at all. It was an accident. Growers wanted tomatoes that ripen evenly. No dark green patch around the stem. The whole field ready on the same day. So breeders selected a variety that goes uniformly pale green before it ripens. And that trait is essentially one gene.

3:20The gene that builds the green machinery

Here is the part nobody expected. That gene builds the green machinery inside the fruit itself. An unripe tomato is not just waiting. It is quietly photosynthesising. It makes part of its own sugar while it hangs on the plant.

3:38Switch it off

Switch that gene off, and you get exactly what was ordered: a tomato that ripens beautifully evenly. You also get less sugar, and less of the pigment that makes it red. Nobody chose that. They chose the way it looks, and the sweetness quietly left with it.

3:58And then the fridge

And then we finish the job at home. The aroma compounds in a tomato are damaged below twelve degrees. And some of the genes that make them never properly switch back on. So the fridge doesn't pause a tomato. It takes something away that doesn't fully come back. And the cucumber has its own version of the same story.

4:21The cucumber did the same thing

A wild cucumber is bitter. Aggressively bitter. It is full of a defence chemical that discourages most things from eating it. Domestication switched that off in the fruit. We bred the bitterness out on purpose, and it worked.

4:38Exactly what we asked for

We asked agriculture for vegetables that are firm, even, cheap, and that don't fight back. We got exactly that.

So if your entire experience with vegetables is supermarket cucumber and supermarket tomato… I can understand why you might conclude:

4:57Vegetables are not one thing

"I don't like vegetables." Maybe you don't dislike vegetables. Maybe you just dislike these two. Because here's the funny thing. Vegetables aren't one thing.

There are dozens and dozens of completely different plants we eat as vegetables. And they don't all taste remotely alike.

5:18Carrots, broccoli and cabbage

Take carrots. You can eat them raw. But roast them with a little oil and they turn sweeter and far more interesting. Or broccoli. You don't have to boil it until it becomes a sad green cloud. Roast it. Grill it. Add garlic. Add some spices. And it becomes an entirely different food. Cauliflower? Same story. Roasted cauliflower can have a surprisingly rich, nutty flavour. And then there is cabbage. Fresh. Cooked. Fermented. Suddenly we're not talking about a boring side dish anymore.

5:56Vegetables with an opinion

Personally, I think vegetables are much more interesting when they actually have an opinion. Radishes. Onions. Garlic. Horseradish. Chilli peppers. These don't sit quietly on your plate. They arrive and announce: "Hello. I have a flavour." And if you like spicy food, chillies are almost cheating. A tiny amount can completely change an otherwise boring meal. And there are endless varieties. Mild ones. Smoky ones. Ridiculously hot ones that exist mainly to make you question your life choices.

6:34Cooking changes everything

This is another thing I find strange. When people talk about eating vegetables, they often imagine them raw. A few slices of cucumber. A tomato. Maybe some lettuce. And that's supposed to be exciting. But cooking changes vegetables dramatically. Roast a beetroot. Grill a pepper. Caramelise an onion. Bake a whole cauliflower. And suddenly vegetables aren't just something sitting beside your actual food. They are the food.

7:08Maybe it was how they were served

And this might actually explain why some people say: "I don't like vegetables." Maybe they were simply introduced to them in the worst possible way. Boiled broccoli. Overcooked cauliflower. Frozen spinach. A cucumber that has spent three days in the fridge. And then someone says: "You need to eat your vegetables." That's not exactly a great marketing campaign. Imagine doing the same thing with potatoes. Boil them without salt. Don't season them. Leave them until they're cold. And then tell a child: "See? Potatoes are healthy." They might never eat a potato again.

7:51You can actually choose

The good news is that you don't need to force yourself to eat vegetables you hate. There isn't a law saying: "Every human must consume exactly one cucumber and half a tomato every evening." You can choose. And the choice is much wider than the three things on the plate. When did anyone last offer you kohlrabi? Or celeriac? Or asparagus? These are not exotic. They are in the same shop, a few steps away, and nobody ever mentions them. And any of them can be eaten raw, roasted, grilled, fermented, pickled, or stuffed into something else entirely.

8:29In defence of tomatoes and cucumbers

And just to be fair… I'm not saying tomatoes and cucumbers are bad. A really good tomato? Fantastic. A fresh cucumber on a hot summer day? Also fantastic. The problem is not that tomatoes and cucumbers exist. The problem is that we have somehow turned them into the default definition of vegetables. As if the vegetable department had two products… and occasionally a pepper gets through security.

8:59What do I actually like?

And there is actually a bigger lesson here. If somebody tells you: "You should eat more vegetables." The answer doesn't have to be: "Oh no. More cucumber." It can be: "What vegetables do I actually like?" Maybe it's roasted carrots. Maybe it's spicy peppers. Maybe it's sauerkraut. Maybe it's something you've never even tried. Because vegetables aren't a punishment. And they certainly don't have to taste like water.

9:28Barely the tutorial

So next time someone puts a plate in front of you containing tomato, cucumber, and, in the better case, a pepper… remember: this isn't the entire vegetable kingdom. It's barely the tutorial.

And somewhere out there there is a vegetable you might actually enjoy eating. You just have to find it. If you want the next one, there is a subscribe button.
