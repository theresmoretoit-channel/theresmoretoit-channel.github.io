Why Would Canada Join the European Union?

10 min2026-09-17

But Canada is already halfway inside. It joined SAFE, the Union's joint arms programme, in February 2026, as the first country from outside Europe. It joined the EU research programme in 2024, two years after Israel and New Zealand. Goods have crossed the Atlantic under a joint trade agreement since 2017.

So geography guards the front door. It never guarded the side entrances.

The full narration of the video.

0:00Canada wants to join the European Union

Have you heard? Canada wants to join the European Union. What a nonsense. How is Canada supposed to move to Europe? Except that in 2026, the president of the European Commission stood up in the European Parliament and proposed more or less exactly that. Not as a joke. As policy.

0:22Something that does not exist yet

Not full membership. Something that does not exist yet. Canada as the Union's first associate member.

And here is the part most people miss. Canada is already halfway in.

0:37Canada is already in SAFE

In February 2026, Canada joined something called SAFE. That is the Union's joint scheme for arming itself. A hundred and fifty billion euros of borrowed money, spent on weapons together. Canada is the first country outside Europe ever allowed in. That did not come out of nowhere. The summer before, Canada and the Union signed a security and defence partnership.

1:04Research money and a trade deal

In 2024, Canada joined Horizon Europe, the Union's research programme. Canadian labs now apply for European money. And since 2017, goods have crossed the Atlantic under a joint trade agreement. So the question was never whether Canada can work with Europe. It already does.

The question is how far this goes. And that turns out to be a question about three words in a treaty.

1:34The rule about who may join

The European Union has a rule about who may join. It is short. Any European State… may apply to become a member of the Union. Any European State. Canada is a great many things. A European state is not one of them.

Although notice something. That sentence is about membership.

1:59Israel and New Zealand are already in

The research programme Canada joined never said anything of the kind. Israel has been in it for years. So has New Zealand. Geography guards the front door. It never guarded the side entrances.

So what is actually being proposed?

2:19Picture two doors

Picture two doors. Behind the first one: full membership. A seat at the table. A vote. A veto. And every obligation that comes with it. Under the treaties as they stand, that door is shut. Behind the second one: an ordinary partner. Trade deals. Joint projects. Everything Canada already has. That door is wide open. It just doesn't take you anywhere new.

2:46A third door

So Europe is talking about building a third door. One that isn't in the building yet. The list of what might go through it is already circulating. Defence. Energy. Critical minerals. Artificial intelligence. Quantum technology. Cybersecurity. And the Arctic.

But the moment you draw a new door, you have to say how wide it opens.

3:12How wide does it open?

How much of the single market would Canada get? Which European rules would Canada have to follow? Would Canadian firms sell into Europe on the same terms as German ones? Would Canada pay in? And then the one that decides everything. Would Canada get a say in the rules it has to obey? And this is not hypothetical.

3:36A carbon bill on imported steel

Europe has started charging imported steel and cement for the carbon burnt to make them. A Canadian smelter that wants to sell into Europe now fills in its paperwork under a rule written in Brussels. It pays. It does not vote.

3:54Norway said no. Twice.

Norway is not in the European Union. Norwegians voted against joining. Twice. But since 1994, Norway has been inside most of the single market. Norwegian goods move freely. Norwegian citizens move freely. Norway pays into the budget.

4:14Thirteen thousand rules

And Norway takes on European rules. More than thirteen thousand of them since 1994. Written in Brussels. Voted on in Brussels. By people Norwegians do not elect. Norwegians have a phrase for it. Fax democracy. The rules arrive. You sign.

4:35The staircase between in and out

So Europe already comes in more than two sizes. There is in. There is out. And there is a whole staircase in between. But every step on that staircase has been standing in Europe. Canada would be the first one standing somewhere else.

5:00Why would Europe want this?

Which raises the obvious question. Why would Europe want that? Start with the map, and then keep zooming out. Canada reaches far into the Arctic.

5:12The longest coastline on Earth

It has the longest coastline of any country on Earth, and most of it faces ice. As that ice thins, the ocean underneath turns into shipping lanes, seabed and borders that nobody has finished arguing about. Europe has Arctic coastline too. It would rather not argue alone.

Then there is what sits under the Canadian ground.

5:37Nickel, cobalt, copper

Nickel. Cobalt. Copper. Rare earth elements. The unglamorous metals inside every battery, every turbine in the North Sea, every guided missile. Europe has the factories and almost none of the ore. And Europe has spent four years learning what it costs to buy something essential from a single supplier.

6:00The view from Ottawa

Now turn the map around and look at it from Ottawa. Canada sells about three quarters of everything it exports to one country. The United States.

6:11A dependency with a long border

That is not a trading relationship. That is a dependency with a very long border. And Canada has spent recent years discovering that the terms of that relationship can change without warning.

6:25Europe is doing the same arithmetic

And Europe has been having a version of the same conversation with itself. Spending more on defence. Asking out loud how much of its own security it ought to be able to provide. Neither of them says it loudly. Both of them are doing the arithmetic.

Canada is not going to replace the United States with Europe. That is not the proposal. It doesn't have to. It just has to be less alone in the room.

6:57The awkward questions

So both sides want this. Which is exactly when it is worth slowing down. Because the awkward questions have not gone anywhere. Imagine a European law that lands on a Canadian company.

7:11It follows the rule. It did not vote.

Canada follows the rule. Canada did not vote on the rule. Is that acceptable? Now flip it. Imagine Canada getting real influence over European decisions. Would a voter in Portugal accept that a country on another continent helped write the law that binds them?

7:32How many kinds of membership?

And if the answer to both is a carefully worded maybe, how many different kinds of membership can one union hold before nobody can say what membership means? That is the whole argument. Who is in? And who decides?

7:49Back to the name

Which brings us back to the name. The European Union. Those two words only ever run in one direction. European, therefore Europe, therefore European countries. But look at what the second word is doing. Union. A union is not a place. It is an agreement about what its members do together.

8:12What the Union actually is

Think about what the European Union actually is. Common rules. A common market. Shared courts. Money moving without permission. Standards written once and obeyed in twenty-seven countries. Not one of those things is inherently European. A country on the other side of the Atlantic can have compatible courts. It can meet the same standards. It can agree to the same rules.

8:42Six neighbours, 1951

The project began in 1951 with six neighbours and an agreement about coal and steel. Neighbours was the starting point. The question is whether it is also the ceiling.

8:55Nothing has been agreed

Nothing has been agreed. This is a proposal, and proposals in Brussels have a long and difficult life ahead of them. There is a summit in October that is supposed to turn the idea into something with edges. Canada may end up with a slightly better trade deal and nothing more. But the interesting part already happened. Europe said out loud that membership might not have to mean what it has always meant. Canada does not have to move to Europe. Europe does not have to move to Canada.

9:27Seventy-five years, one question

For seventy-five years, European integration answered one question. How do we get European countries to cooperate? The next question is different. How do we get countries to cooperate when they are not European at all?

9:43What the U could still mean

Maybe the E tells us where the European Union came from. And maybe the U tells us what it could still become.
