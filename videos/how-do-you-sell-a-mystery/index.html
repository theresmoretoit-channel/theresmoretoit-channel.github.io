How Do You Sell A Mystery?

11 min2026-09-21

Does that prove his theories about ancient aliens were a deliberate fraud? No. It proves he committed fraud. Those are two different claims, and the gap between them is what this video is about.

Because the interesting thing about Erich von Däniken isn't whether he was honest. It's that his method still works, and it has three steps: start with something real, point at something we don't fully understand, and ask whether the answer might be extraordinary. The first two steps can be completely true. That is exactly what makes the third one land.

The full narration of the video.

0:00A way of telling a story

There is a way of telling a story that makes almost anything look like a mystery. It has three steps. Once you have seen them, you cannot unsee them. They are in documentaries, in headlines, in entire TV channels.

The man who perfected the method sold seventy million books. Before we get to those three steps, there is something you should know.

0:24A hotel manager, arrested

In November 1968, Swiss police arrested Erich von Däniken. Not for anything he had written. For forging hotel paperwork. He had faked invoices and credit references for twelve years to take out loans worth a hundred and thirty thousand dollars. He spent the money on travel. On the very journeys described in the book that had come out that March. In 1970 a court gave him three and a half years for repeated and sustained fraud. He wrote his next book in prison.

0:58Two different claims

So. Does that prove his theories about ancient aliens were a deliberate fraud? No. It proves he committed fraud. Those are two different claims. And the gap between them… the jump you almost made a second ago… is what this whole video is about. Because that jump is the move he made for a living. Not once. Thousands of times, in front of millions of readers.

1:27How do you sell a mystery?

So let's take it apart. How do you sell a mystery?

Step one. Start with something real.

1:36Step one: something real

Not a hoax. Not a forgery. Something a reader can visit, photograph and measure. The Nazca Lines, for instance. They are real. They are enormous. Hundreds of lines and figures scraped into a desert plateau in southern Peru. Step one is free. You have not claimed anything yet. You have simply pointed at a thing that exists.

1:59Step two: something unexplained

Step two. Point at something we do not fully understand. And here you still do not have to lie. Why people made lines best appreciated from the air is a real archaeological question. Two steps in, every single word has been true. That is what makes the method work. The trouble, if any, comes next.

2:21Step three: something extraordinary

Step three. Ask whether the explanation might be something extraordinary. Perhaps they were landing strips.

Notice what happened there. Nobody claimed anything. A question was asked. And questions cannot be wrong.

2:39What archaeologists found

Meanwhile the lines do not need visitors at all. Archaeologists found the wooden stakes at their ends. They found offerings left on them. Pottery. Textiles. And that most of the figures are drawn with one unbroken line. The technique is not exotic either. Stakes, cord, and the patience to clear dark stones off the pale ground underneath. But nothing in the archaeology suggests a landing strip. And notice what that took. We did not have to explain every line out there to say it. Rejecting one explanation is not the same as having all the answers.

But that answer takes a minute, and the question took four words. Hold on to that, because it comes back later.

And the shape repeats everywhere. The pyramids. Mayan monuments. Old religious texts. Real thing. Open question. Aliens?

3:37Take Palenque

Take Palenque. The lid of a Maya ruler's sarcophagus shows a man who looks remarkably like somebody sitting inside a machine. Däniken saw an astronaut. A control panel. An engine. A spacecraft. Archaeologists see the tomb of Pakal the Great, who ruled that city for sixty-eight years and died in the seventh century. They see a world tree. The open jaws of a funerary serpent. A king wearing the attributes of the Maya maize god.

4:08What the reading skipped

And their objection is not that it looks nothing like a rocket. It is that the rocket reading comes from looking, and nothing else. Not the burial. Not the inscriptions. Not everything else those same symbols meant on every other Maya carving. The object is real. The strangeness is real. The extra step is the reading.

4:32So was he lying?

So was Däniken simply lying?

The evidence does not give us a comfortable answer. A con man takes the money and stops. Däniken kept going for six decades. He kept writing and arguing long after he was rich. In 2018, aged eighty-three, he said contact would come within ten years. And in the same interview: "For this I have no proof."

He died in January 2026, aged ninety. That does not look like a simple one-time publishing scam. And yet the conviction is real, and it does tell us one thing. He was willing to falsify documents when it suited him. Both of those can be true at once, and that is the uncomfortable part.

5:25The method doesn't need a liar

Because the method does not need a liar. It works perfectly well in the hands of a believer. A believer picks the best examples. A believer leaves out the inconvenient context. And can mislead people without ever thinking of it as lying. And that is the distinction the whole thing turns on. Believing something sincerely, and handling evidence honestly, are two completely different skills. You can have the first and fail the second without ever telling a lie.

6:07Television arrives

And then television arrived and discovered something worse. The method does not need a believer either.

Ancient Aliens has been running since 2010. The same three steps. Every week. Find something impressive. Note something unexplained. Move past the ordinary explanation. Ask whether it could be aliens. Add ominous music. Cut to the sky. Go to the advert break. Then do it again.

6:39Making the answer dull

And here is the part that makes it a business. The show does not have to prove aliens. It only has to make the answer dull. Picture an episode about an ancient monument. The exciting version takes twenty seconds. Nobody knows exactly how every stone was moved. Therefore… aliens? The ordinary version needs generations of accumulated skill, thousands of workers, ramps, ropes and organisation. It needs the archaeology. The workers' village beside the pyramids. Their bakeries. Their healed broken bones. It needs the experiments in which people actually moved blocks weighing several tonnes using wood, water and rope.

It is a better answer. It is also much harder to turn into an hour of television.

7:32Twenty seconds against three hours

Which brings back the thing I asked you to hold on to. There is a documentary called Ancient Aliens Debunked. It takes the claims one at a time. It runs for three hours and eleven minutes. Twenty seconds to raise the question. Three hours to answer it properly. No wonder the question wins.

7:56One note about that film

One note about that film. Its maker, Chris White, comes from a Christian apologetics background. Worth knowing. And it does not make the archaeology in it wrong. Any more than a fraud conviction makes his argument about Nazca wrong. That argument fails on the archaeology, not on his record. Judge the evidence, not the person holding it. A rule that only runs one way was never a rule.

I will put a link to the documentary below.

8:30What became of his idea

Which brings us back to Däniken, and to something genuinely odd about what became of his idea. He was a traveller. A storyteller. He was honestly fascinated by ancient civilisations. His explanations were poorly supported. Often demonstrably wrong. His methods were criticised for nearly sixty years. In one book he printed a photograph of what he called markings like the parking areas of a modern airport. It was a cropped picture of the knee joint of one of the Nazca birds.

But he did at least take the reader somewhere. Egypt. Peru. Mexico. You could feel the dust. Ancient Aliens took that idea and built a machine out of it. Find something strange. Leave out what does not fit. Ask whether aliens could explain it. Add the music. And do it again next week. So in a strange way, the show does not preserve his legacy. It cheapens it. The man had one extraordinary idea.

Television discovered that the idea did not need an answer. It only needed another episode.

9:49We already have people

So the next time something asks you whether it could be, you already know which step you are standing on. And there is nothing wrong with saying we do not know. It is often the most honest sentence available. It is not a hole that something has to fill. It is the reason the ancient world is worth studying. We do not need aliens. We already have people. And people are strange enough. Because the mystery was never really the aliens. The mystery is why we kept watching.
