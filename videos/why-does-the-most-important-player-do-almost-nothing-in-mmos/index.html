Why Can Tanking Be Surprisingly Easy?

11 min2026-09-18

Which is odd, because a lot of the time the job is to walk up to the boss, press taunt, and stand there. The damage dealers are holding rotations and tracking buffs. The healer is watching health bars. The tank is blocking, waiting, and accepting that other people are going to make mistakes that are not its problem.

Games have noticed how few people want it. World of Warcraft once added Call to Arms, a bag of extra rewards for whichever role the queue was short of. Final Fantasy XIV still has its own version, Adventurer in Need. Whole reward systems, built to talk somebody into a role nobody volunteered for.

0:00Three roles, and one nobody picks

Imagine joining a multiplayer game for the first time. In games like World of Warcraft, Final Fantasy XIV or The Elder Scrolls Online, groups are built around three basic roles. You can deal damage. You can heal your teammates. Or you can become the tank. The person who stands directly in front of the thing everyone else is trying very hard not to stand in front of. And of the three, the tank is often the one nobody wants to be.

0:27The role nobody wants

Which is a little odd. Because apparently, this is a very important job. The boss is attacking you. Your teammates are depending on you. If you make a mistake, someone might die. If you die, everyone might die. And if the group wipes? Well… Everyone knows whose fault it is. So you might expect tanking to be incredibly difficult. But here's the strange thing. Sometimes… it really isn't.

Let's look at a typical boss fight. The damage dealers are busy. They have rotations to maintain. Abilities to time.

1:04What the damage dealers are doing

Resources to manage. Buffs and debuffs to track. They need to stay out of dangerous areas. And they need to do all of that while watching the boss for the next mechanic. The healer has their own problems. They need to keep everyone alive. Watch health bars.

1:26What the healer is doing

Manage their resources. And anticipate when the next big hit is coming. And then there's the tank.

1:37And then there is the tank

The tank walks towards the boss. Uses a taunt. And… stands there. That's it.

Well, not exactly. The tank needs to keep the boss pointed away from the group. Block the big attacks. Move when necessary. Keep the boss where the group wants it. And occasionally deal with some adds. But once everything is under control… there can be an astonishing amount of nothing happening.

2:05An astonishing amount of nothing

The boss hits you. You block. The boss hits you again. You block again. Your abilities come off cooldown. You press them. And then… you wait. Meanwhile, your damage dealers are performing what looks like a small piano recital on their keyboards.

2:21A small piano recital

Now imagine one of your damage dealers makes a mistake. They stand in something they shouldn't. They die. What does the tank do? Usually… nothing.

The boss is still attacking the tank. The healer can resurrect them. The group can continue. In fact, a surprisingly large part of being a tank is accepting that other people are going to make mistakes.

2:48Other people will make mistakes

And that's not necessarily your problem. A dead damage dealer is unfortunate. A dead tank is a completely different situation. Because the boss immediately finds somebody who cannot take it. And that's when things become interesting.

The tank isn't really there to keep everyone alive. That's the healer's job.

3:15Not keeping everyone alive

The tank's job is to control the encounter. The boss should be looking at you. The boss should be standing where you want it. Its dangerous attacks should be directed away from everyone else. And when the boss does something particularly unpleasant… you should be the one standing there saying:

3:34Whenever you are ready

"Whenever you are ready."

This is why tanking can feel strangely relaxing. You're not trying to kill the boss as quickly as possible. You're trying to make the fight predictable. And predictable fights are much easier to survive. A role can be extremely important without requiring you to constantly do complicated things.

3:58Important without being complicated

Imagine a machine with ten components. Nine of them are doing something all the time. One is mostly sitting there. But if that one component fails… the whole machine stops. That doesn't mean the component is complicated. It means it's critical.

Tanking often works exactly like this. The tank may have fewer things to do than the damage dealers.

4:34Fewer things, bigger consequences

But some of those things have enormous consequences. Press the wrong button as a DPS? Maybe you lose some damage. Miss a tank mechanic at the wrong moment? The boss may kill you. And now the entire group has a problem. Unless there is a second tank. And still, hardly anyone puts their hand up.

5:01Games pay people to tank

Games have noticed. World of Warcraft once added Call to Arms: a bag of extra rewards for whichever role the queue was short of. Final Fantasy XIV has its own version, and it even has a name. Adventurer in Need. Entire reward systems, built to bribe somebody into a role nobody volunteered for.

So if tanking can be this simple, why does it feel so intimidating?

5:29So why is it intimidating?

When you're a damage dealer, you can often make a mistake quietly. Your damage drops. Maybe nobody even notices. But when you're the tank, your mistakes are visible. The boss suddenly turns around. Someone gets hit. The group wipes. And you immediately know: "Yeah. That was me." That's enough to make a perfectly competent player think: "I don't want to be responsible for this." You don't actually need to be perfect.

6:01Reliable, not perfect

You need to be reliable.

A tank who does five things consistently is worth more than one who knows twenty tricks and forgets the basics. And there's another reason tanking is more forgiving than it looks. A tank is usually built to survive.

6:15Built to survive

Lots of health. High resistances. Damage mitigation. Shields. Blocking. The whole character is designed to be difficult to kill. Damage dealers are often the exact opposite. Because DDs are usually glass cannons.

6:36Glass cannons

They're built to turn as much of their survivability as possible into damage. And that creates a funny situation. A tank can take a bad hit and lose some health. A damage dealer takes the same hit… and suddenly they're a corpse. So while the tank may have more responsibility for the encounter, the tank often has more room for error.

6:58More room for error

Which is probably not what you'd expect. The role that looks the most dangerous… may actually be one of the most forgiving.

But there is a catch to it.

7:15But there is a catch

Everything I've described so far works beautifully… until the encounter becomes difficult. Now the tank may have to position the boss precisely. Move it at specific moments. Control additional enemies. Interrupt abilities. Manage resources. Swap weapons.

7:28Everything a hard fight asks for

Swap with the other tank before the debuffs stack up and kill you. Use defensive abilities before the damage arrives. And sometimes perform mechanics that nobody else in the group can. Now the tank isn't just standing there. The tank is planning.

And this is where tanking changes. You're no longer reacting to what the boss just did. You're thinking about what the boss is going to do next.

7:57Reacting turns into planning

So maybe we can think about tanking as three different jobs. At the first level: "Don't let the boss attack everyone else." That's surprisingly easy. At the second: "Control the battlefield." Now you're positioning enemies, managing adds and helping the group deal damage safely. And at the highest level: "Understand the entire encounter." You know what's coming. You know where the boss needs to be. You know when the group needs your help. And you're effectively conducting the fight. The first level doesn't have to be terrifying just because the third one is difficult.

You don't learn to drive by entering a Formula One race.

8:46You do not start in Formula One

And you don't need to tank the hardest raid in the game to find out whether you enjoy tanking.

There is also something tanking gives you that damage dealing often doesn't.

8:57What tanking gives you

Time.

More time to look at the encounter. More time to observe the boss. More time to understand what's happening. You're not squeezing every last percent of performance out of yourself. You're trying to maintain control. And once you've achieved that control… you can sometimes just stand there. The boss hits you. You block. Your teammates do their thing. And you think: "Wait. Is this really my job?" Yes. That is the job.

We often assume that the most important person in a team must also be the busiest person. But that's not necessarily true.

9:38Important is not the same as busy

Sometimes the most valuable thing you can do is make sure one very important thing doesn't go wrong. The tank doesn't have to kill the boss. The tank doesn't have to heal everyone. The tank doesn't even have to prevent every mistake. The tank simply has to make the fight manageable. Predictable. Controlled. And when everything goes well… it can look like the tank is doing almost nothing. Which is perhaps the ultimate compliment.

10:09Doing almost nothing

Because when the tank is doing their job perfectly… the rest of the group gets to do theirs. And maybe that's why tanking isn't as scary as it looks. You aren't carrying the entire group. You're just standing in exactly the place where the boss wants to kill someone. And making sure… it's you.
