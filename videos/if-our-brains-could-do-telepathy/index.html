Other Minds

If Our Brains Could Do Telepathy

16 min2026-08-29

The answer turns out to be much bigger than "stronger brain waves". Your brain really does produce electromagnetic activity — we can measure it with electrodes on the scalp. But a transmitter with nobody able to receive it is just a rock next to a radio. And even a perfect receiver would not help, because a thought is not a signal: there is no single neuron in your head that says "apple".

Work through it properly and telepathy stops being one ability and becomes an entire communication system — a transmitter, a receiver, structure, a decoder, a filter, and an address. That is not a change to one gene. That is a whole new sense.

The full narration of the video.

0:00Two people, one thought

Imagine sitting in a room with somebody else. You say nothing. You are not even looking at each other. And yet you know exactly what the other person is thinking. Not because you can read their face. Not because you know them well. You simply know. A thought passes from one brain into another. This is what we call telepathy. And although people have talked about it for hundreds of years, nobody has ever reliably shown that a human brain can do anything of the kind. But today we are going to do something a little different. We are not going to ask whether telepathy exists.

We are going to ask something else. What would have to be different for telepathy to be biologically possible? And we may find that stronger brain signals would not be enough. Because telepathy would not be one ability. It would be an entire communication system.

0:58The brain already transmits

Our brain is not electrically dead. Quite the opposite. Every second, an enormous number of electrical and chemical processes are running inside it. Neurons pass information to each other using electrical impulses and chemical messengers. And because electric currents create electromagnetic fields, the brain produces electromagnetic activity too. We can even measure it. Electrodes resting on the scalp pick up the rhythms of the cortex. So there is a sensible question here. Could one brain send information out, and another brain receive it?

But this is where we run into a fundamental problem. The fact that something is transmitting a signal does not mean that anybody can receive it.

1:48A transmitter with no receiver

Picture an ordinary radio. A transmitter can broadcast at, say, one hundred megahertz. But if you put a rock beside it, the rock will hear no music. Not because the signal is not there. Because the rock has no receiver. And the idea of natural telepathy has very much the same problem. Even if we somehow amplified the electromagnetic signals of the brain, we would still need a biological receiver. Something able to catch the signal. And then something just as important. Something able to recognise what the signal means.

2:27A signal is not a thought

This is perhaps the biggest problem of the whole idea. Suppose we could measure a person's brain activity perfectly. We see billions of neurons. We see which regions are active. We see the electrical signals. And still none of that tells us what the person is thinking. Because a thought is not a simple electrical signal. When I think about an apple, there is no single neuron in my brain that says, apple. Individual cells can be surprisingly picky about what they respond to. But not one of them carries the whole thought.

It is an enormous combination of neural activity. It is tied to the shape of the apple. Its colour. Its taste. Its smell. Memories. Language. Emotions. And above all, the whole of my previous life. And that is exactly why every brain is a little bit different.

3:24What a telepathic language needs

So if evolution wanted to build telepathy, it would have to solve something much like the invention of language. There would have to be structure. Not a secret alphabet where one pattern means danger and another one means food. Something looser than that. A pattern that another brain could learn to recognise. Because a receiver does not have to translate symbols one at a time. Your eye does not turn photons into some universal code before you can see. It is simply built, and then trained, to read whatever arrives. Which opens a rather nice question. Would two telepaths have to learn how to speak to each other? Or would part of that language already be there at birth?

With ordinary animal signals, it is usually both. A vervet monkey is born already knowing how its alarm calls sound. But it has to learn what each one of them is for. And step by step, a real language of the brain could come into being. And this is where evolution becomes genuinely interesting. Because a language does not have to be built out of sound. We humans use sound mainly because we have vocal cords and ears. Another species could use light. Ultrasound. Chemicals. Electrical signals. Or, in principle, electromagnetic signals.

4:49What if a brain had an aerial

So let us imagine a completely different creature. Part of its brain is a structure that works as a biological antenna. Something like our ear, except that instead of sound it picks up electromagnetic signals. And another part of the brain works as a transmitter. Suddenly we have a chain. One brain transmits. The signal crosses the space between. The other brain receives. That is no longer magic. That is a communication system. And if such a system lasted long enough, evolution could start improving it. Better sensitivity. Greater range. Better filtering of noise. Faster decoding. And in the end, perhaps very complex communication.

5:38Why would it ever evolve?

But why would any of this evolve in the first place? That is an important question. Evolution has no plan. It does not build abilities because they would be interesting. An ability spreads when it improves the chances of surviving or reproducing. And telepathy could be an enormous advantage. Picture a group of hunters. No need to shout. No need to wave. No need to look at each other at all. The whole group could coordinate almost instantly. Or a parent could warn its young about a predator, without making a single sound.

Or you could talk in the dark. Behind an obstacle. In a dense forest. The advantages could be huge.

6:23What water does to the idea

Although the physics would have something to say about it. Take water. Sea water conducts electricity, and it swallows radio waves. The higher the frequency, the shorter the reach. That radio from a moment ago would be gone within a few centimetres of sea. Which is why our own submarines listen on extremely low frequencies, and why a three-letter message could take a quarter of an hour. So a species in the ocean would not simply have telepathy. It would have the kind that its water allowed. Slow. Short. Simple. The medium would be shaping the mind that used it.

7:03A million minds at once

And then comes the problem. Picture a city where everybody has telepathy. And everybody is using it at once. It would not be a polite how are you today. It would be a million brains transmitting a million signals. It would be complete chaos. So a telepathic brain would need something we already have ourselves. A filter. Our own brain does not consciously take in everything either. We do not notice every sound. Every touch. Every smell. Every image. The brain chooses what to pay attention to. There would have to be a mechanism that says, this thought I want to send. And at the same time, this one I do not.

7:46Where do you even send a thought?

And underneath that one sits a second problem. You think about someone. But how would your brain know where to send the thought? Radio has a frequency. A telephone has a number. The internet has an address. A telepathic mind would need something of the same kind. So does every mind carry a signature of its own? A frequency? A pattern that nobody else has? Or would you have to do something stranger than that, and somehow tune yourself to one particular mind? Nature already does half of this. The electric pulse of a knife fish carries its species, its sex, and quite probably which fish it is. An identity, written into the signal.

8:34Telepathic privacy

And here telepathy starts to get frightening. Because if evolution built telepathy, it would probably have to build something like telepathic privacy. Imagine somebody who can hear all your thoughts. Not only the ones you mean to say out loud. But also whatever crosses your mind for half a second. Random associations. Memories. Doubts. Fear. Wishes. Thoughts that do not mean that you actually want them. Suddenly it would matter enormously to separate what I am thinking from what I want to tell you. And perhaps this is where one of the most important abilities of a telepathic brain would appear. A mental firewall.

9:18The Trisolarans

And science fiction has already run this experiment. Not the experiment of how telepathy might work. A different one. What it would do to a society. In the trilogy Remembrance of Earth's Past, by the writer Liu Cixin, there is a species called the Trisolarans. They cannot conceal their thoughts the way that people can. For them, thinking something and saying it are not two different acts. For a human that is almost unimaginable. We are used to an enormous gap between what I think, what I say, and what I actually do. Take that gap away, and a great deal of what we build on top of it goes with it. Lying. Bluffing. Diplomacy. Even keeping a secret to be kind.

10:05Would an implant count?

And now an interesting question. Suppose we built an implant that reads the activity of your brain… turns it into data… sends that data across the internet… and a second implant turns it back into activity in another brain. Is that telepathy? Strictly speaking, no. It is technological communication between brains. But for the person using it? The difference might be almost invisible. You do not have to speak. You do not have to type. You do not have to look at a screen. You simply think, send me that photograph. And the other person receives it.

And this may be the more realistic road. We do not have to wait for evolution to grow a biological antenna. Technology could do the same thing a great deal faster. The implant can be our antenna. The computer can be the translator. The internet can be the channel. And a second implant can be a loudspeaker wired straight into a brain. Suddenly we have something that, from the point of view of the user, begins to look a great deal like telepathy. But we would not have to send whole thoughts. We could send only a piece of one.

11:21It has already been done

And that is exactly how it started. Because a crude version of this has already been done. No implants. Just electrodes and a magnetic coil. More than ten years ago, a person in India imagined moving their hands. Electrodes picked it up. The signal travelled across the internet. And in France, a magnetic pulse at the back of somebody's head made them see a flash of light. A flash meant one. No flash meant zero. A four-letter word took a hundred and forty of those. The receiving brain saw flashes. It never knew that anything had been said. The computer put the word together afterwards.

Since then, three people have played a shared game of falling blocks that way, brain to brain. One bit each, one turn at a time. It is slow. It is clumsy. But it is real. And that is probably the road. A signal. Then a word. Then an image. Then a concept. And perhaps, one day, a whole mental state.

12:27A planet built on it

But let us go back to evolution. Imagine a planet where life has used this kind of communication from the very beginning. Animals with biological transmitters and biological receivers. Predators that hunt without a sound. Prey that can warn the others. Social groups that can share what they know. And slowly, an intelligent species. Its first word was never spoken. Because it never needed words. Its language was not made of sound. It was mental. And its culture would have grown in a completely different direction from ours.

13:07No such thing as a secret

Perhaps there would be no such thing as a secret. Imagine a civilisation where sharing your thoughts is the natural thing to do. Lying could be far harder. But a different kind of manipulation would appear instead. Because even if you cannot hide a thought… you may still be able to shape how the other one reads it. So there would be telepathic propaganda. Telepathic advertising. Telepathic art. Perhaps even telepathic war. And of course, a telepathic password. Because if somebody can read your mind, you need a way of deciding who you actually let in.

13:47Six things we would need

So what would we really need? If we wanted genuine biological telepathy, we would need at least six things. First, a transmitter. The brain would have to make a signal that carries information. Second, a receiver. Another brain would have to catch it. Third, structure. The signal would have to be something a brain could learn. Fourth, a decoder. The brain would have to recognise what it was reading. Fifth, a filter. It would have to decide what to send and what to keep. And sixth, an address. It would have to know which mind it was talking to.

And that is not a simple change to one gene. That is practically a whole new sense.

14:35Would we still be individuals?

So if we discovered tomorrow that human brains transmit far stronger electromagnetic signals, that would not automatically mean that we had discovered telepathy. It would only mean that we have a transmitter. We would still need a receiver. We would need structure and decoding. We would need a way to aim it. And above all we would need a way to turn the electrical activity of one brain into something that another brain genuinely recognises as a thought. And that is exactly why the idea of telepathy is so fascinating. Because real telepathy would not be a stronger brain wave. It would not even be one single ability. It would be a whole system.

And in the end, a new way of perceiving the world. Just as we have sight, hearing, smell and touch, we would have one more sense. Another mind. And perhaps the most interesting question begins right there. If we could one day link human brains with technology, would we still be individuals? Or would we slowly start turning into something that we cannot even name today?
