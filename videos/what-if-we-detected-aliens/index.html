We Found Aliens. Now What?

12 min2026-09-07

We would be looking for technosignatures across the whole electromagnetic spectrum, not just radio. We might read a planet's atmosphere without ever going there. And then distance takes over: at five hundred light-years, one question and one answer cost a thousand years, so the first reaction would not be "let's call them" but "record everything".

Even with the signal in hand, we would still have to work out what it means — which turns out to be a bit like trying to hold a conversation with a dolphin. Dolphins are intelligent, they communicate, we have recorded them for decades, and we share a planet, a biosphere and a physical world with them. It is still extremely hard. Now remove all of that.

The full narration of the video.

0:00A signal nothing in nature explains

Imagine that tomorrow, astronomers detect something strange. Not just an unusual radio signal. Not a pulsar. Not a quasar. Not some new natural phenomenon. Something that looks like technology. Something that is changing in a way nature doesn't normally do. Someone made it.

And that would mean that somewhere out there, another technological civilisation exists — or existed. But then comes the really interesting question. How would we actually communicate with them?

0:38First we have to find them

But before we can communicate with them, we have to find them. When we think about extraterrestrial communication, we usually imagine radio. But there is no reason to assume that an alien civilisation would use exactly the technology we use. So we should be looking for technosignatures across the electromagnetic spectrum. Radio and microwaves. Optical and laser signals. Infrared radiation. And even the chemical fingerprints of planetary atmospheres. We don't have to visit a planet to learn what it is made of. We can look at its light.

The missing colours in a spectrum tell us which molecules absorbed them. And an atmosphere full of industrial gases could be evidence of technology. We might never see the civilisation itself. We might simply see what its technology does to its planet.

1:35Five hundred light-years

Now suppose we actually find something. There is a civilisation five hundred light-years away. That sounds relatively close on a galactic scale. But it means that the signal we are receiving left them five hundred years ago. If we send a message back, it takes another five hundred years to arrive. And then their reply, another five hundred. One question. One answer. A thousand years.

If they are a thousand light-years away, the round trip takes two thousand years. Across the Galaxy, we could be talking about tens of thousands of years. At that point, this isn't really a conversation anymore. It is correspondence between civilisations separated by centuries or millennia. We wouldn't be talking to them. We would be talking to a civilisation that existed when the signal was sent. They might already be gone. Or they might have changed completely. So realistically, if we detect a technosignature on the other side of the Galaxy, our first reaction probably won't be this. "Let's call them."

It will be this. "Record everything."

2:53We still have to work out what it means

Even if the signal is clearly artificial, we still have to figure out what it means. We might recognise that there is information encoded in it. We might find repeating patterns. We might even discover something that looks remarkably like a grammar. But none of that tells us what the symbols mean. And this is where things get really strange.

3:17The dolphin problem

Because communicating with an alien civilisation might be a little like trying to communicate with dolphins. We know dolphins are intelligent. We know they communicate with each other. We can record their sounds and look for patterns. We can study their behaviour for decades. And yet, we still don't have anything resembling a normal conversation with them. And dolphins are right here. We share the same planet. We evolved in the same biosphere. We can watch what they do. We can experiment. And even with all of that, understanding their communication is extremely difficult.

Now imagine doing the same thing with an alien civilisation. Except we don't share a planet. We don't share an evolutionary history. We may not share the same senses. We may not even share the same basic concepts. And they could be hundreds or thousands of light-years away. Suddenly, communicating with aliens starts to look less like calling another civilisation… …and more like trying to have a conversation with a dolphin that lives in another galaxy.

4:31Everything you understood without being told

There is another reason human communication works so well. We share an enormous amount of common knowledge. If I tell you this. "A dog is sitting on the table." You immediately understand a ridiculous amount of information that I didn't actually say. You know what a dog is. You know what a table is. You understand objects, gravity, bodies and perspective. And if I show you a photograph of that dog, you also understand what a photograph is. You understand that the thing inside the image isn't actually inside your computer.

None of this needs to be explained. Because we share the same world. An alien doesn't.

5:17They might not think like us

Their language might not have nouns and verbs. Maybe those categories simply don't make sense in their way of thinking. Maybe their communication isn't a sequence of symbols at all. Perhaps it is spatial. Or visual. Or based on changes in electromagnetic fields. Maybe they communicate things that we don't even have concepts for. And the problem works in both directions. They might be looking at our transmissions and thinking. "What on Earth is this?" And that would be a perfectly reasonable question. Suppose we transmit a photograph of a dog. But what are they actually receiving? A pattern of electromagnetic radiation.

We could spend years explaining the concept of a dog… …only to discover that the alien equivalent of "dog" isn't even a meaningful category in their world.

6:17A different sense of time

Biology also determines how fast organisms process information. An alien civilisation could have an entirely different subjective timescale. Imagine a species whose thoughts happen a thousand times faster than ours. Our idea of a "conversation" might be meaningless to them.

6:38Maybe there is no conversation

And this may be the most disappointing possibility. Even if we discover intelligent extraterrestrial life… …we may never have a conversation with it. We might only ever receive messages from its past. We could spend generations trying to understand a signal that was transmitted before our civilisation even existed. But that doesn't make the signal useless. Quite the opposite. It could be the most valuable scientific object humanity has ever discovered. Something made by another intelligence.

7:16What an AI could actually do

This is where artificial intelligence becomes extremely interesting. An AI wouldn't necessarily understand the alien message. But it could be extraordinarily good at finding structure. Give it enough data and it could search for repetitions. Patterns. Correlations. Hierarchies. Possible symbols. Possible grammar. It could test millions of hypotheses that would take humans centuries to explore. And if we had other observations of the same civilisation. Its planet, atmosphere, energy use, orbital structures. We could begin connecting the message with the physical world.

Not because AI magically understands aliens. But because it can search an enormous space of possibilities far faster than humans can.

8:07Why we would never delete the data

And if the signal were real, we probably wouldn't keep the problem inside one laboratory. Imagine releasing it to the world. Millions of computers. Scientists. Amateurs. Universities. AI researchers. Different algorithms competing to find patterns in the same signal. It could become something like SETI@home on steroids. And there would be one thing we would absolutely not want to do. Delete the data.

We would want the original observations. Every frequency. Every spectrum. Every timing detail. Everything. Because the analysis techniques of the future might find something that we completely missed today. We might end up with an astronomical archive containing more raw data than any scientific project in human history.

9:02They do not have to send us science

And suppose the aliens aren't trying to teach us physics. Suppose they send us entertainment. A soap opera. Advertisements. Sports. Music. News. Something completely mundane to them. It could be incredibly valuable to us. A soap opera might reveal relationships. Social hierarchies. Humour. Jealousy. What they fear. What they desire. An advertisement might tell us what they value. A sports broadcast might tell us about competition and cooperation. And suddenly, something that was completely ordinary to them becomes an extraordinary window into an alien civilisation.

And of course… …they could do exactly the same thing to us. And if our television broadcasts ever reached them, they might be trying to figure out why humans spend so much time watching people fall in love, buy cars, fight wars and kick a ball around.

10:05The distance is still there

But even after all of this… …we still have the distance problem.

AI might help us decode the message. We might eventually understand their language. We might even learn astonishing things about their science. But if they are a thousand light-years away, we still can't have a normal conversation. The universe doesn't care how good our translators become. Light still takes a thousand years to cross that distance.

10:38What first contact might really look like

So first contact might not look anything like science fiction. There might be no face-to-face meeting. No alien landing. No handshake. Perhaps first contact is simply a faint signal arriving at a telescope… …followed by decades or centuries of people trying to understand it. And maybe, generations later, someone finally figures out what it says. Not because the aliens came to us. But because, for the first time in human history, we discovered evidence that somewhere in the universe…

…someone else was there.

And that alone would change everything.
