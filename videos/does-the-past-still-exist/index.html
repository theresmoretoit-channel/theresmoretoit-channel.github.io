Physics That Sounds Simple Until You Ask

Does the Past Still Exist?

10 min2026-09-12

This is not a video about what time is. It is about a much narrower question: if something is no longer happening, what does it mean to say that it exists?

Everything we have of the past is a trace. A photograph is not the past — it is an object sitting in the present that happens to carry information about it. A fossil is not the dinosaur. Ash is not the tree. And most of what has ever happened left nothing at all.

The full narration of the video.

0:00Where is yesterday?

What happened yesterday? You woke up. You had breakfast. You talked to someone. Maybe you went to work. And now? None of those things are happening anymore. But they happened. You know they happened. So here is a strange question. Where is yesterday now? Is it somewhere in the universe? Did it simply disappear? Or does the past still exist, in some sense, even though we can no longer experience it?

Before we can answer that, we need a much smaller question first. What does it mean for something to exist?

0:36A photograph is not the past

Suppose you have a photograph of yourself from twenty years ago. The photograph exists. It is sitting on your computer right now. But the person in that photograph? That particular moment of your life is not happening anymore. The photograph is not the past. It is an object in the present, carrying information about the past. And once you notice that, you start seeing it everywhere.

1:03Fossils, ash, memory

A fossil is not the dinosaur. Ash is not the tree that burned. A memory in your head is not yesterday. They are all traces. Marks left behind by something that happened.

1:17Traces are thin

And traces are thin. Ash tells you that there was a tree. It does not tell you which tree, or how tall it stood, or what the light looked like underneath it. Most of what happens leaves nothing at all. What colour was the sky at three o'clock last Tuesday? How many steps did you take the day before that? Both of those were real events. They had a definite answer. And now there may be no trace of it anywhere. Which gives us an uncomfortable possibility. Maybe the past does not exist. Maybe all that exists is the small amount it happened to leave behind.

1:58The Sun, eight minutes ago

Look at the Sun. What you see is not the Sun as it is right now. You see the Sun as it was about eight minutes ago. That is simply how long its light takes to reach us. And it gets much more extreme than that.

2:13A galaxy, a billion years ago

When you look at a galaxy a billion light years away, you are seeing it as it was a billion years ago. Not a picture of it. Not a recording of it. That is the actual light, arriving today, after a journey of a billion years. So astronomy is not really the study of distant things. It is the study of old things. The farther away we look, the deeper into the past we see.

2:39Even this room

The screen in front of you is a few nanoseconds in the past. The far wall of the room, a little further back than that. You have never once seen anything in the present. Everything you have ever seen is a record of something that already happened.

So the universe is full of information about its own past. But look carefully at what that information is. It is light arriving today. A signal in the present. And information about the past is still not the past itself.

3:15There is no universal now

Now there is a much bigger problem. If light takes time to travel, what does right now even mean for the whole universe? What is happening right now on a planet a thousand light years away? Relativity gives us no single answer. Two observers moving differently can disagree about which distant events happen at the same time. Neither of them is wrong. Neither of them is measuring badly. There is no single universal now shared by the entire universe. And that is awkward for us. Because if there is no universal present, what exactly separates the past from it?

3:57The block universe

One possible answer is called the block universe. Imagine a film. Watching it, you get the story one frame at a time. Beginning. Middle. End. But the film itself is a complete strip of frames. The ending does not come into existence at the moment you reach it. It was there the whole time. The block universe is a little like that. Instead of a universe being built moment by moment, picture the whole of its history as one four-dimensional structure. Past events are part of it. Present events are part of it. Future events are part of it. Nothing has to move through it. The structure simply is.

4:40Your childhood in spacetime

On that picture, your childhood is not something that vanished. It is a different part of spacetime. Which is a strange kind of comfort. And also a strange kind of threat. Because it would mean that tomorrow's version of you is already there.

4:56Relativity does not prove it

But be careful here. This is one possibility. Relativity makes it attractive. Relativity does not prove it.

Because there are other answers, and philosophers have names for them.

5:12Presentism

The first one is presentism. Only the present exists. The past is gone. The future has not happened yet. It matches how everything feels. But it has to answer a hard question. If observers cannot agree on what is happening now, what exactly is the present?

5:32Eternalism

The second one is eternalism. Past, present and future all exist. Our sense of now is just where we happen to be standing. That fits naturally with relativity. But it has to explain why we only ever experience one thin slice of it, and always in the same direction.

5:52The growing block

The third one sits between them. It is called the growing block. The past exists. The present exists. The future does not. Reality is a block that keeps getting bigger. Which sounds reasonable, until you ask where its edge is. And relativity gives us no universal way to define it.

6:13Physics made it harder

Notice what physics has done here. It did not settle the argument. It made the argument harder. Relativity makes a single universal now very difficult to defend. But relativity does not tell us what the word exist means. That part is still philosophy.

6:35What are we claiming?

And it is worth asking honestly. Suppose we say that yesterday's breakfast exists. What are we actually claiming? Is it somewhere in space? Can we go there? Can we measure it? Can anything about it still change? Not in any way we can access. The only thing we can point at is information available today. Which takes us straight back to the photograph. The photo is not the event. The memory is not the event. The light from a galaxy is not the galaxy's past. And yet every one of them points at something that really did happen.

7:15A question that pulls them apart

So here is a question that pulls apart two ideas we normally keep glued together. Imagine that you wake up tomorrow and every record of yesterday is gone. No photographs. No documents. No memory of it, in any head. No physical trace anywhere, down to the last atom.

7:35If every trace were gone

Would yesterday have stopped existing?

Or would it still have happened, even though nothing in the universe remembered that it did? And yet we already assume this, quietly, all the time.

7:50A conversation in the Bronze Age

Somebody in a Bronze Age village had a conversation one evening and then went to bed. Nothing recorded a word of it. We do not conclude that the conversation never took place. We conclude that we cannot reach it. And that is two different things.

8:07Existence and evidence

Whether the past exists. And whether there is any evidence that it does. Those are not the same question. And almost everything that feels obvious about time quietly assumes that they are.

8:23So, does it?

So. Does the past still exist? Maybe not. Maybe only the present is real, and the universe keeps making new moments. Maybe it does. Maybe the whole history of everything sits there in spacetime, and today is simply where we are standing. Or maybe the difference between gone and still there does not work the way we think it does. We don't know.

8:49They happened

But there is something oddly reassuring about one of those answers. Maybe the things that happened to us do not have to keep happening in order to be real. They happened. And perhaps that is enough.
