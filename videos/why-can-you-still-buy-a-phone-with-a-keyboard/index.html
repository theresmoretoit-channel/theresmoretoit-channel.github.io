Why Can You Still Buy a Phone With a Keyboard?

8 min2026-10-21

Touchscreens won this fight more than fifteen years ago. So why does a phone like this still exist? And who would actually want one?

From BlackBerry to the Unihertz Titan, the Astro Slide, the F(x)tec Pro1 X and the Clicks Communicator: what can physical keys do that glass can't, why don't Samsung, Apple or Google make phones like this — and is the keyboard on a phone really dead?

The full narration of the video.

0:00A new phone with a keyboard

This is a smartphone you can buy today. 5G. A recent version of Android. Twelve gigabytes of memory, five hundred and twelve gigabytes of storage. And a physical QWERTY keyboard. Real keys. Ones you actually press. Not a ten-year-old BlackBerry from eBay. A new phone. Touchscreens won this fight more than fifteen years ago. So why does this thing even exist? And who would want one? There's a rather unexpected answer to that. But we'll get there.

0:35BlackBerry

To understand it, we need to go back to BlackBerry. BlackBerry didn't invent the keyboard on a phone. But it turned the idea into a phenomenon. Tiny screen, tiny buttons, and a full QWERTY keyboard underneath. The phone felt almost like a tiny computer. You could type without covering half the screen with your fingers. For people who lived in email, that was a big deal. BlackBerry became the phone of business, messaging and productivity.

1:06Then came the iPhone

Then the iPhone came along. And the touchscreen allowed one device to become almost anything. The keyboard didn't have to be permanently attached. It could change language. It could change layout. It could disappear completely when you didn't need it. And most importantly, the entire front of the phone could become a screen. So the physical keyboard gradually disappeared. BlackBerry itself eventually stopped making its own phones. A company that later tried to revive the brand with a new keyboard phone never actually released one.

1:43The end of the story?

By the early 2020s, it really did look like the story was over. Except… while Apple, Samsung and everyone else were making increasingly similar glass rectangles… a few small companies were doing something completely different.

2:00Unihertz Titan

One of them is the small smartphone manufacturer Unihertz, with its Titan series. It's been making them since 2019. The idea is remarkably simple. Take an Android smartphone… and put a physical keyboard underneath the screen. The Titan 2 is the phone from the start of this video. Not a Bluetooth keyboard. Not an accessory. The keys are part of the phone. And the newer Titan 2 Elite comes with Android 16 and a hundred and twenty hertz AMOLED display. The keyboard can even act as a kind of touchpad. You can move the cursor, scroll through pages, and assign shortcuts to keys.

So this isn't an old BlackBerry brought back to life. It's a modern Android phone… that happens to have a keyboard.

2:49Not the only one

And Unihertz isn't alone. Planet Computers made the Astro Slide. Its screen slid upward and revealed a mechanical keyboard underneath. It was much closer to a tiny computer than to a traditional smartphone. The keyboard was backlit, programmable, and came in many language layouts, including Czech, Swedish and Danish. Then there was F(x)tec. Their Pro1 X looked like an ordinary touchscreen phone… until it slid open to reveal a full keyboard. It came out in 2022, so it's hardly a current flagship. But it shows the idea was never limited to a single company.

And new projects keep appearing, such as the Clicks Communicator. So there is clearly still a market for this idea. Tiny… but real.

3:48Why physical keys?

But why? Because there is something a touchscreen keyboard can't completely reproduce. You can feel a physical key. When you type on glass, your finger doesn't know where the next letter is. You have to look. With physical keys, your fingers find their position. You feel the gap between keys. You feel when a key was pressed. And once you've learned the layout, you can sometimes type while looking at something else. That's a completely different way of using a phone. It's the same reason physical keyboards never disappeared from computers.

Nobody asks, "Why do programmers still use keyboards? We already have touchscreens." Because sometimes physical controls are simply better. Not for everything. But for some things.

4:38Keys as shortcuts

And on a modern phone, a keyboard doesn't just mean typing. The keys can work as shortcuts. Press one key to open an app. Hold another to run an action. Swipe across the keys to scroll. So the keyboard becomes another input device, not just a way of entering letters. These phones aren't trying to recreate the past. They combine an old interface with a modern operating system.

5:07Problem one: space

So if it's that useful, why is it so rare? The first problem is space. Every millimetre of a smartphone is valuable. Put a keyboard under the screen, and you need a bigger phone. Or a smaller screen. Or both. That's a hard sell when everyone else is competing to make phones thinner, lighter, and almost entirely screen.

5:31Problem two: language

The second problem is language. A physical keyboard needs a different layout for each one. QWERTY. QWERTZ. AZERTY. Cyrillic. And many others. A touchscreen doesn't care. Change the software, and you've changed the keyboard. With physical keys, you have to manufacture something different. And suddenly your market gets much smaller.

5:54It's economics

So it's not that the technology doesn't work. It works perfectly well. The problem is economics.

Millions of people want a large screen. Millions want a good camera. Millions want a thin phone. But how many people specifically want a physical keyboard? Far fewer. That's why Samsung doesn't make a phone like this. Apple doesn't make one. Google doesn't make one. Instead, you get small companies making unusual phones for people who want something different. And perhaps that's enough.

6:33Is the keyboard dead?

So is the physical keyboard really dead? Well… not quite. It's no longer mainstream. You won't find it in a phone shop. And you're certainly not going to see a new BlackBerry on the shelf. But the idea didn't disappear. It became a niche. And sometimes that's exactly how technologies survive. They don't have to win. They just have to remain useful to somebody.

7:02The unexpected answer

And that unexpected answer from the start? This script was written on a phone with a physical keyboard. This one.

And so were the scripts for a lot of other videos on this channel. The physical keyboard didn't really die. It just became something you have to look for. And if you really want one, you can still buy one today.
