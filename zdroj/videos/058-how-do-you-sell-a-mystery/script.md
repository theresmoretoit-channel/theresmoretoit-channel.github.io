<!-- S01 -->
There is a way of telling a story
that makes almost anything
look like a mystery.
It has three steps.

<!-- S02 -->
Once you have seen them,
you cannot unsee them.
They are in documentaries,
in headlines, in entire TV channels.

<!-- S03 -->
The man who perfected the method
sold seventy million books.
Before we get to those three steps,
there is something you should know.

<!-- S04 -->
In November 1968,
Swiss police arrested Erich von Däniken.
Not for anything he had written.
For forging hotel paperwork.

<!-- S05 -->
He had faked invoices
and credit references for twelve years
to take out loans worth
a hundred and thirty thousand dollars.

<!-- S06 -->
He spent the money on travel.
On the very journeys
described in the book
that had come out that March.

<!-- S07 -->
In 1970 a court gave him
three and a half years
for repeated and sustained fraud.
He wrote his next book in prison.

<!-- S08 -->
So.
Does that prove his theories
about ancient aliens
were a deliberate fraud?

<!-- S09 -->
No.
It proves he committed fraud.
Those are two different claims.

<!-- S10 -->
And the gap between them...
the jump you almost made
a second ago...
is what this whole video is about.

<!-- S11 -->
Because that jump is the move
he made for a living.
Not once. Thousands of times,
in front of millions of readers.

<!-- S12 -->
So let's take it apart.
How do you sell a mystery?

<!-- S13 -->
Step one.
Start with something real.

<!-- S14 -->
Not a hoax. Not a forgery.
Something a reader can visit,
photograph and measure.
The Nazca Lines, for instance.

<!-- S15 -->
They are real. They are enormous.
Hundreds of lines and figures
scraped into a desert plateau
in southern Peru.

<!-- S16 -->
Step one is free.
You have not claimed anything yet.
You have simply pointed
at a thing that exists.

<!-- S17 -->
Step two.
Point at something
we do not fully understand.

<!-- S18 -->
And here you still do not have to lie.
Why people made lines
best appreciated from the air
is a real archaeological question.

<!-- S19 -->
Two steps in,
every single word has been true.
That is what makes the method work.
The trouble, if any, comes next.

<!-- S20 -->
Step three.
Ask whether the explanation
might be something extraordinary.

<!-- S21 -->
Perhaps they were landing strips.

<!-- S22 -->
Notice what happened there.
Nobody claimed anything.
A question was asked.
And questions cannot be wrong.

<!-- S23 -->
Meanwhile the lines
do not need visitors at all.
Archaeologists found
the wooden stakes at their ends.

<!-- S24 -->
They found offerings left on them.
Pottery. Textiles.
And that most of the figures
are drawn with one unbroken line.

<!-- S25 -->
The technique is not exotic either.
Stakes, cord, and the patience
to clear dark stones
off the pale ground underneath.

<!-- S26 -->
But nothing in the archaeology
suggests a landing strip.

<!-- S27 -->
And notice what that took.
We did not have to explain
every line out there to say it.
Rejecting one explanation

is not the same as
having all the answers.

<!-- S28 -->
But that answer takes a minute,
and the question took four words.
Hold on to that,
because it comes back later.

<!-- S29 -->
And the shape repeats everywhere.
The pyramids. Mayan monuments.
Old religious texts.
Real thing. Open question. Aliens?

<!-- S30 -->
Take Palenque.
The lid of a Maya ruler's sarcophagus
shows a man who looks remarkably like
somebody sitting inside a machine.

<!-- S31 -->
Däniken saw an astronaut.
A control panel.
An engine.
A spacecraft.

<!-- S32 -->
Archaeologists see the tomb
of Pakal the Great,
who ruled that city for sixty-eight years
and died in the seventh century.

<!-- S33 -->
They see a world tree.
The open jaws of a funerary serpent.
A king wearing the attributes
of the Maya maize god.

<!-- S34 -->
And their objection is not
that it looks nothing like a rocket.
It is that the rocket reading
comes from looking, and nothing else.

<!-- S35 -->
Not the burial. Not the inscriptions.
Not everything else
those same symbols meant
on every other Maya carving.

<!-- S36 -->
The object is real.
The strangeness is real.
The extra step is the reading.

<!-- S37 -->
So was Däniken simply lying?

<!-- S38 -->
The evidence does not give us
a comfortable answer.

<!-- S39 -->
A con man takes the money and stops.
Däniken kept going for six decades.
He kept writing and arguing
long after he was rich.

<!-- S40 -->
In 2018, aged eighty-three,
he said contact would come
within ten years.
And in the same interview:

<!-- S40b -->
"For this I have no proof."

<!-- S40c -->
He died in January 2026,
aged ninety.

<!-- S41 -->
That does not look like
a simple one-time publishing scam.

<!-- S42 -->
And yet the conviction is real,
and it does tell us one thing.
He was willing to falsify documents
when it suited him.

<!-- S43 -->
Both of those can be true at once,
and that is the uncomfortable part.

<!-- S44 -->
Because the method
does not need a liar.
It works perfectly well
in the hands of a believer.

<!-- S45 -->
A believer picks the best examples.
A believer leaves out
the inconvenient context.
And can mislead people without

ever thinking of it as lying.

<!-- S46 -->
And that is the distinction
the whole thing turns on.
Believing something sincerely,
and handling evidence honestly,

<!-- S47 -->
are two completely different skills.
You can have the first
and fail the second
without ever telling a lie.

<!-- S47b -->
By the way, if you'd like to see
where the next question takes us,
consider subscribing.

It really helps the channel.

<!-- S48 -->
And then television arrived
and discovered something worse.

<!-- S49 -->
The method does not need
a believer either.

<!-- S50 -->
Ancient Aliens has been running
since 2010.
The same three steps.
Every week.

<!-- S51 -->
Find something impressive.
Note something unexplained.
Move past the ordinary explanation.
Ask whether it could be aliens.

<!-- S52 -->
Add ominous music.
Cut to the sky.
Go to the advert break.
Then do it again.

<!-- S53 -->
And here is the part
that makes it a business.
The show does not have to prove aliens.
It only has to make the answer dull.

<!-- S54 -->
Picture an episode
about an ancient monument.
The exciting version
takes twenty seconds.

<!-- S55 -->
Nobody knows exactly
how every stone was moved.
Therefore...
aliens?

<!-- S56 -->
The ordinary version needs
generations of accumulated skill,
thousands of workers,
ramps, ropes and organisation.

<!-- S57 -->
It needs the archaeology.
The workers' village beside the pyramids.
Their bakeries.
Their healed broken bones.

<!-- S58 -->
It needs the experiments
in which people actually moved
blocks weighing several tonnes
using wood, water and rope.

<!-- S59 -->
It is a better answer.
It is also much harder
to turn into an hour of television.

<!-- S60 -->
Which brings back
the thing I asked you to hold on to.

<!-- S61 -->
There is a documentary
called Ancient Aliens Debunked.
It takes the claims
one at a time.

<!-- S62 -->
It runs for three hours
and eleven minutes.

<!-- S63 -->
Twenty seconds to raise the question.
Three hours to answer it properly.
No wonder the question wins.

<!-- S64 -->
One note about that film.
Its maker, Chris White,
comes from a Christian
apologetics background.

<!-- S65 -->
Worth knowing.
And it does not make
the archaeology in it wrong.

<!-- S66 -->
Any more than a fraud conviction
makes his argument about Nazca wrong.
That argument fails
on the archaeology, not on his record.

<!-- S67 -->
Judge the evidence,
not the person holding it.
A rule that only runs one way
was never a rule.

<!-- S68 -->
I will put a link
to the documentary below.

<!-- S69 -->
Which brings us back to Däniken,
and to something genuinely odd
about what became of his idea.

<!-- S70 -->
He was a traveller.
A storyteller.
He was honestly fascinated
by ancient civilisations.

<!-- S71 -->
His explanations were poorly supported.
Often demonstrably wrong.
His methods were criticised
for nearly sixty years.

<!-- S71b -->
In one book he printed a photograph
of what he called markings
like the parking areas
of a modern airport.

<!-- S71c -->
It was a cropped picture
of the knee joint
of one of the Nazca birds.

<!-- S72 -->
But he did at least
take the reader somewhere.
Egypt. Peru. Mexico.
You could feel the dust.

<!-- S73 -->
Ancient Aliens took that idea
and built a machine out of it.
Find something strange.
Leave out what does not fit.

<!-- S74 -->
Ask whether aliens could explain it.
Add the music.
And do it again next week.

<!-- S75 -->
So in a strange way,
the show does not
preserve his legacy.
It cheapens it.

<!-- S76 -->
The man had one extraordinary idea.

<!-- S77 -->
Television discovered
that the idea did not need an answer.
It only needed another episode.

<!-- S78 -->
So the next time something
asks you whether it could be,
you already know
which step you are standing on.

<!-- S79 -->
And there is nothing wrong
with saying we do not know.
It is often the most honest
sentence available.

<!-- S80 -->
It is not a hole
that something has to fill.
It is the reason
the ancient world is worth studying.

<!-- S81 -->
We do not need aliens.
We already have people.

<!-- S82 -->
And people are strange enough.

<!-- S83 -->
Because the mystery
was never really the aliens.

<!-- S84 -->
The mystery is why we kept watching.
