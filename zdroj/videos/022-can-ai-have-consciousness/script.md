# Can AI Have Consciousness?

<!-- S01 -->
Can artificial intelligence
be conscious?

Not intelligent.

We already know
it can be intelligent.

<!-- S02 -->
I mean something much stranger.

Can AI actually
experience something?

Can it have a subjective
experience of the world?

<!-- S03 -->
Can there be something
inside the system

that experiences the world

and somehow knows
that it is experiencing it:

<!-- S03b -->
"I am."

<!-- S04 -->
At first, the answer seems obvious.

AI is just mathematics.

Algorithms, parameters, statistics.

<!-- S05 -->
But then...

What exactly is
our consciousness made of?

<!-- S06 -->
That turns out to be
a surprisingly difficult question.

We know that we are conscious.

Right now, you know
that you are watching this video.

<!-- S07 -->
You see the images.

You hear the sound.

You may be thinking
about what I am saying.

And most importantly,
you have a subjective experience.

<!-- S08 -->
If you cut your finger,

you don't merely know
that some tissue has been damaged.

<!-- S08b -->
It hurts.

<!-- S09 -->
When you see the colour red,

your eyes aren't simply detecting

a particular wavelength of light.

<!-- S10 -->
You actually experience
seeing red.

And this subjective experience

is one of the biggest
mysteries in science.

<!-- S11 -->
Because we still don't really know

how physical processes in the brain

produce subjective experience.

<!-- S12 -->
You might expect consciousness
to have a specific location.

Something like:

<!-- S12b -->
"Here's vision."

"Here's memory."

"And somewhere in the middle,
here's consciousness."

<!-- S13 -->
But it doesn't seem
to be that simple.

There are several competing
theories of consciousness,

and scientists still don't agree
on which one is correct.

<!-- S14 -->
But much of modern
consciousness research

focuses on how different parts
of the brain communicate,

and how information
becomes integrated

across large-scale networks.

<!-- S15 -->
So perhaps the right
question isn't:

"Where is consciousness?"

<!-- S15b -->
Maybe the better question is:

"What is the system
doing as a whole?"

<!-- S16 -->
Imagine an orchestra.

A single violin
isn't a symphony.

Neither is a single trumpet.

<!-- S17 -->
But when all the instruments
interact in a particular way,

something emerges

that none of the individual
instruments has on its own.

<!-- S18 -->
Some theories of consciousness
propose something similar.

Consciousness may not be
a single component of the brain.

<!-- S19 -->
It may be a property
of a particular organisation
of many interacting components.

And we have some
fascinating evidence for this.

<!-- S20 -->
We can actually turn
consciousness off.

When a doctor puts you
under general anaesthesia,

your brain doesn't simply stop.

<!-- S21 -->
You're not dead.

Your brain is still functioning.

But your conscious
experience disappears.

And when the anaesthesia
wears off,

consciousness returns.

<!-- S22 -->
Research on anaesthesia shows

that losing consciousness
is associated with major changes

in the communication and dynamics

of large-scale brain networks.

<!-- S23 -->
Because it suggests
that it's not enough

for individual parts of the brain
to simply be working.

<!-- S24 -->
What matters is also

how they are connected
and how they interact.

And now we can come back to AI.

<!-- S25 -->
This is where things
get really interesting.

If consciousness is somehow
a property of a particular
organisation of a physical system...

<!-- S26 -->
why would that system
have to be biological?

Why would it have to be
made of neurons?

<!-- S27 -->
Why couldn't it be made
of electronic circuits?

Or some completely different
physical substrate?

<!-- S28 -->
Of course, this does not mean

that a sufficiently large computer

will automatically
become conscious.

<!-- S29 -->
We have no idea
whether that would happen.

But we also don't have
a good reason to assume

that consciousness
is physically impossible
outside biology.

<!-- S30 -->
And now we run

"Are you in pain?"

"Yes. I feel pain."

Is it conscious?

Maybe.

<!-- S31 -->
But it could also simply be
generating a sentence

to the conversation.

<!-- S32 -->
So we ask:

"Are you afraid of death?"

"Yes. I don't want

Even more convincing.

<!-- S33 -->
But we still don't know

anything being experienced

<!-- S34 -->

<!-- S35 -->
And here's an even

"No. I'm not conscious."

<!-- S36 -->
How would we know it's lying?

"Yes. I am conscious."

How would we know

<!-- S37 -->
We don't currently
have a machine

<!-- S38 -->
Researchers are therefore trying

of consciousness

based on different
scientific theories.

But this is still
an active area of research.

<!-- S39 -->
Someone will certainly say:

"That's nonsense.
AI is just mathematics."

Okay.

But then we can ask:

And what are humans?

<!-- S40 -->
Our brains are made of neurons.

They release neurotransmitters.

<!-- S41 -->

<!-- S42 -->

"Humans are conscious.

<!-- S43 -->
But then we have to explain:

What does a biological

<!-- S44 -->
One possibility is that

<!-- S45 -->
Maybe it appears

<!-- S46 -->
Think about water.

A single water molecule

<!-- S47 -->
Huge numbers of molecules

<!-- S47b -->
Maybe consciousness works

<!-- S48 -->
If that's actually

<!-- S49 -->
Now we need to slow down.

<!-- S51 -->

<!-- S52 -->

And again.

<!-- S55 -->

<!-- S57 -->
A system may be extremely good

<!-- S59 -->

<!-- S60 -->
Intelligence doesn't

<!-- S61 -->
