<!-- S01 -->
Imagine that tomorrow,
astronomers detect
something strange.

<!-- S02 -->
Not just an unusual radio signal.
Not a pulsar.
Not a quasar.
Not some new natural phenomenon.

<!-- S03 -->
Something that looks like technology.
Something that is changing
in a way nature doesn't normally do.

<!-- S04 -->
Someone made it.

<!-- S05 -->
And that would mean
that somewhere out there,
another technological civilisation
exists — or existed.

<!-- S06 -->
But then comes
the really interesting question.

<!-- S07 -->
How would we actually
communicate with them?

<!-- S08 -->
But before we can
communicate with them,
we have to find them.

<!-- S09 -->
When we think about
extraterrestrial communication,
we usually imagine radio.
But there is no reason to assume
that an alien civilisation
would use exactly
the technology we use.

<!-- S10 -->
So we should be looking
for technosignatures
across the electromagnetic spectrum.
Radio and microwaves.
Optical and laser signals.
Infrared radiation.

<!-- S11 -->
And even the chemical fingerprints
of planetary atmospheres.
We don't have to visit a planet
to learn what it is made of.
We can look at its light.

<!-- S12 -->
The missing colours in a spectrum
tell us which molecules
absorbed them.
And an atmosphere full
of industrial gases
could be evidence of technology.

<!-- S13 -->
We might never see
the civilisation itself.
We might simply see
what its technology does
to its planet.

<!-- S14 -->
Now suppose we actually find something.
There is a civilisation
five hundred light-years away.
That sounds relatively close
on a galactic scale.

<!-- S15 -->
But it means that the signal
we are receiving
left them five hundred years ago.
If we send a message back,
it takes another five hundred years
to arrive.
And then their reply,
another five hundred.

<!-- S16 -->
One question.
One answer.
A thousand years.

<!-- S17 -->
If they are a thousand
light-years away,
the round trip
takes two thousand years.
Across the Galaxy,
we could be talking about
tens of thousands of years.

<!-- S18 -->
At that point, this isn't really
a conversation anymore.
It is correspondence
between civilisations
separated by centuries or millennia.

<!-- S19 -->
We wouldn't be talking to them.
We would be talking to a civilisation
that existed when the signal was sent.
They might already be gone.
Or they might have changed completely.

<!-- S20 -->
So realistically,
if we detect a technosignature
on the other side of the Galaxy,
our first reaction
probably won't be this.
"Let's call them."

<!-- S21 -->
It will be this.
"Record everything."

<!-- S22 -->
Even if the signal
is clearly artificial,
we still have to figure out
what it means.

<!-- S23 -->
We might recognise
that there is information
encoded in it.
We might find repeating patterns.
We might even discover something
that looks remarkably
like a grammar.

<!-- S24 -->
But none of that tells us
what the symbols mean.
And this is where
things get really strange.

<!-- S25 -->
Because communicating
with an alien civilisation
might be a little like trying
to communicate with dolphins.

<!-- S26 -->
We know dolphins are intelligent.
We know they communicate
with each other.
We can record their sounds
and look for patterns.
We can study their behaviour
for decades.

<!-- S27 -->
And yet, we still don't have anything
resembling a normal conversation
with them.

<!-- S28 -->
And dolphins are right here.
We share the same planet.
We evolved in the same biosphere.
We can watch what they do.
We can experiment.

<!-- S29 -->
And even with all of that,
understanding their communication
is extremely difficult.

<!-- S30 -->
Now imagine doing the same thing
with an alien civilisation.
Except we don't share a planet.
We don't share
an evolutionary history.

<!-- S31 -->
We may not share the same senses.
We may not even share
the same basic concepts.
And they could be hundreds
or thousands of light-years away.

<!-- S32 -->
Suddenly, communicating with aliens
starts to look less like
calling another civilisation...

<!-- S33 -->
...and more like trying to have
a conversation with a dolphin
that lives in another galaxy.

<!-- S34 -->
There is another reason
human communication works so well.
We share an enormous amount
of common knowledge.

<!-- S35 -->
If I tell you this.
"A dog is sitting on the table."

<!-- S36 -->
You immediately understand
a ridiculous amount of information
that I didn't actually say.
You know what a dog is.
You know what a table is.
You understand objects, gravity,
bodies and perspective.

<!-- S37 -->
And if I show you a photograph
of that dog, you also understand
what a photograph is.
You understand that the thing
inside the image
isn't actually inside your computer.

<!-- S38 -->
None of this needs to be explained.
Because we share the same world.

<!-- S39 -->
An alien doesn't.

<!-- S40 -->
Their language might not have
nouns and verbs.
Maybe those categories
simply don't make sense
in their way of thinking.

<!-- S41 -->
Maybe their communication
isn't a sequence of symbols at all.
Perhaps it is spatial.
Or visual.
Or based on changes
in electromagnetic fields.

<!-- S42 -->
Maybe they communicate things
that we don't even
have concepts for.
And the problem works
in both directions.
They might be looking
at our transmissions and thinking.
"What on Earth is this?"

<!-- S43 -->
And that would be
a perfectly reasonable question.

<!-- S44 -->
Suppose we transmit
a photograph of a dog.
But what are they actually receiving?
A pattern
of electromagnetic radiation.

<!-- S45 -->
We could spend years explaining
the concept of a dog...

<!-- S46 -->
...only to discover
that the alien equivalent of "dog"
isn't even a meaningful category
in their world.

<!-- S47 -->
Biology also determines how fast
organisms process information.
An alien civilisation could have
an entirely different
subjective timescale.

<!-- S48 -->
Imagine a species whose thoughts
happen a thousand times faster
than ours.
Our idea of a "conversation"
might be meaningless to them.

<!-- S49 -->
And this may be
the most disappointing possibility.
Even if we discover
intelligent extraterrestrial life...

<!-- S50 -->
...we may never
have a conversation with it.
We might only ever receive
messages from its past.

<!-- S51 -->
We could spend generations
trying to understand a signal
that was transmitted
before our civilisation even existed.

<!-- S52 -->
But that doesn't make
the signal useless.
Quite the opposite.
It could be the most valuable
scientific object
humanity has ever discovered.

<!-- S53 -->
Something made
by another intelligence.

<!-- S54 -->
This is where
artificial intelligence
becomes extremely interesting.
An AI wouldn't necessarily
understand the alien message.
But it could be extraordinarily good
at finding structure.

<!-- S55 -->
Give it enough data
and it could search for repetitions.
Patterns.
Correlations.
Hierarchies.
Possible symbols.
Possible grammar.

<!-- S56 -->
It could test millions of hypotheses
that would take humans
centuries to explore.

<!-- S57 -->
And if we had other observations
of the same civilisation.
Its planet, atmosphere,
energy use, orbital structures.
We could begin
connecting the message
with the physical world.

<!-- S58 -->
Not because AI magically
understands aliens.
But because it can search
an enormous space of possibilities
far faster than humans can.

<!-- S59 -->
And if the signal were real,
we probably wouldn't keep
the problem inside one laboratory.
Imagine releasing it to the world.
Millions of computers.
Scientists.
Amateurs.
Universities.
AI researchers.

<!-- S60 -->
Different algorithms competing
to find patterns in the same signal.
It could become something like
SETI@home on steroids.
And there would be one thing
we would absolutely
not want to do.

<!-- S61 -->
Delete the data.

<!-- S62 -->
We would want
the original observations.
Every frequency.
Every spectrum.
Every timing detail.
Everything.

<!-- S63 -->
Because the analysis techniques
of the future might find something
that we completely missed today.

<!-- S64 -->
We might end up with
an astronomical archive
containing more raw data
than any scientific project
in human history.

<!-- S65 -->
And suppose the aliens aren't
trying to teach us physics.
Suppose they send us entertainment.

<!-- S66 -->
A soap opera.
Advertisements.
Sports.
Music.
News.
Something completely mundane to them.
It could be
incredibly valuable to us.

<!-- S67 -->
A soap opera
might reveal relationships.
Social hierarchies.
Humour.
Jealousy.
What they fear.
What they desire.

<!-- S68 -->
An advertisement might tell us
what they value.
A sports broadcast might tell us
about competition and cooperation.

<!-- S69 -->
And suddenly, something
that was completely ordinary to them
becomes an extraordinary window
into an alien civilisation.

<!-- S70 -->
And of course...

<!-- S71 -->
...they could do exactly
the same thing to us.

<!-- S72 -->
And if our television broadcasts
ever reached them,
they might be trying
to figure out
why humans spend so much time
watching people fall in love,
buy cars, fight wars
and kick a ball around.

<!-- S73 -->
But even after all of this...

<!-- S74 -->
...we still have
the distance problem.

<!-- S75 -->
AI might help us
decode the message.
We might eventually
understand their language.
We might even learn
astonishing things
about their science.

<!-- S76 -->
But if they are
a thousand light-years away,
we still can't have
a normal conversation.
The universe doesn't care
how good our translators become.

<!-- S77 -->
Light still takes a thousand years
to cross that distance.

<!-- S78 -->
So first contact might not look
anything like science fiction.
There might be
no face-to-face meeting.
No alien landing.
No handshake.

<!-- S79 -->
Perhaps first contact
is simply a faint signal
arriving at a telescope...

<!-- S80 -->
...followed by decades or centuries
of people trying to understand it.

<!-- S81 -->
And maybe, generations later,
someone finally figures out
what it says.

<!-- S82 -->
Not because the aliens came to us.
But because, for the first time
in human history,
we discovered evidence
that somewhere in the universe...

<!-- S83 -->
...someone else was there.

<!-- S84 -->
And that alone
would change everything.
