# Why Can You Still Buy a Phone With a Keyboard?

<!-- S01 -->
This is a smartphone you can buy today.
5G. A recent version of Android.
Twelve gigabytes of memory,
five hundred and twelve gigabytes of storage.

<!-- S02 -->
And a physical QWERTY keyboard.
Real keys. Ones you actually press.

<!-- S03 -->
Not a ten-year-old BlackBerry from eBay.
A new phone.

<!-- S04 -->
Touchscreens won this fight
more than fifteen years ago.
So why does this thing even exist?

<!-- S05 -->
And who would want one?
There's a rather unexpected answer to that.
But we'll get there.

<!-- S06 -->
To understand it,
we need to go back to BlackBerry.

<!-- S07 -->
BlackBerry didn't invent
the keyboard on a phone.
But it turned the idea into a phenomenon.

<!-- S08 -->
Tiny screen, tiny buttons,
and a full QWERTY keyboard underneath.
The phone felt almost like a tiny computer.

<!-- S09 -->
You could type
without covering half the screen
with your fingers.
For people who lived in email,
that was a big deal.

<!-- S10 -->
BlackBerry became the phone
of business, messaging and productivity.

<!-- S11 -->
Then the iPhone came along.
And the touchscreen allowed one device
to become almost anything.

<!-- S12 -->
The keyboard didn't have to be
permanently attached.
It could change language.
It could change layout.
It could disappear completely
when you didn't need it.

<!-- S13 -->
And most importantly,
the entire front of the phone
could become a screen.

<!-- S14 -->
So the physical keyboard
gradually disappeared.
BlackBerry itself eventually stopped
making its own phones.

<!-- S15 -->
A company that later tried to revive the brand
with a new keyboard phone
never actually released one.

<!-- S16 -->
By the early 2020s,
it really did look like
the story was over.

<!-- S17 -->
Except...
while Apple, Samsung and everyone else
were making increasingly similar
glass rectangles...

<!-- S18 -->
a few small companies
were doing something completely different.

<!-- S19 -->
One of them is the small
smartphone manufacturer Unihertz,
with its Titan series.
It's been making them since 2019.

<!-- S20 -->
The idea is remarkably simple.
Take an Android smartphone...
and put a physical keyboard
underneath the screen.

<!-- S21 -->
The Titan 2 is the phone
from the start of this video.
Not a Bluetooth keyboard.
Not an accessory.
The keys are part of the phone.

<!-- S22 -->
And the newer Titan 2 Elite
comes with Android 16
and a hundred and twenty hertz AMOLED display.

<!-- S23 -->
The keyboard can even act
as a kind of touchpad.
You can move the cursor,
scroll through pages,
and assign shortcuts to keys.

<!-- S24 -->
So this isn't an old BlackBerry
brought back to life.
It's a modern Android phone...
that happens to have a keyboard.

<!-- S25 -->
And Unihertz isn't alone.

<!-- S26 -->
Planet Computers made the Astro Slide.
Its screen slid upward
and revealed a mechanical keyboard
underneath.

<!-- S27 -->
It was much closer to a tiny computer
than to a traditional smartphone.
The keyboard was backlit, programmable,
and came in many language layouts,
including Czech, Swedish and Danish.

<!-- S28 -->
Then there was F(x)tec.
Their Pro1 X looked like
an ordinary touchscreen phone...
until it slid open
to reveal a full keyboard.

<!-- S29 -->
It came out in 2022,
so it's hardly a current flagship.
But it shows the idea was never limited
to a single company.

<!-- S30 -->
And new projects keep appearing,
such as the Clicks Communicator.

<!-- S31 -->
So there is clearly still a market
for this idea.
Tiny...
but real.

<!-- S31b -->
By the way, if you like videos
like this, consider subscribing.

It really helps the channel.

<!-- S32 -->
But why?
Because there is something
a touchscreen keyboard
can't completely reproduce.

<!-- S33 -->
You can feel a physical key.

<!-- S34 -->
When you type on glass,
your finger doesn't know
where the next letter is.
You have to look.

<!-- S35 -->
With physical keys,
your fingers find their position.
You feel the gap between keys.
You feel when a key was pressed.

<!-- S36 -->
And once you've learned the layout,
you can sometimes type
while looking at something else.
That's a completely different way
of using a phone.

<!-- S37 -->
It's the same reason
physical keyboards never disappeared
from computers.

<!-- S38 -->
Nobody asks,
"Why do programmers still use keyboards?
We already have touchscreens."

<!-- S39 -->
Because sometimes
physical controls are simply better.
Not for everything.
But for some things.

<!-- S40 -->
And on a modern phone,
a keyboard doesn't just mean typing.
The keys can work as shortcuts.

<!-- S41 -->
Press one key to open an app.
Hold another to run an action.
Swipe across the keys to scroll.

<!-- S42 -->
So the keyboard becomes
another input device,
not just a way of entering letters.

<!-- S43 -->
These phones aren't trying
to recreate the past.
They combine an old interface
with a modern operating system.

<!-- S44 -->
So if it's that useful,
why is it so rare?

<!-- S45 -->
The first problem is space.
Every millimetre of a smartphone
is valuable.

<!-- S46 -->
Put a keyboard under the screen,
and you need a bigger phone.
Or a smaller screen.
Or both.

<!-- S47 -->
That's a hard sell
when everyone else is competing
to make phones thinner, lighter,
and almost entirely screen.

<!-- S48 -->
The second problem is language.
A physical keyboard needs
a different layout for each one.

<!-- S49 -->
QWERTY.
QWERTZ.
AZERTY.
Cyrillic.
And many others.

<!-- S50 -->
A touchscreen doesn't care.
Change the software,
and you've changed the keyboard.

<!-- S51 -->
With physical keys,
you have to manufacture
something different.
And suddenly your market
gets much smaller.

<!-- S52 -->
So it's not that
the technology doesn't work.
It works perfectly well.
The problem is economics.

<!-- S53 -->
Millions of people want a large screen.
Millions want a good camera.
Millions want a thin phone.

<!-- S54 -->
But how many people
specifically want a physical keyboard?
Far fewer.

<!-- S55 -->
That's why Samsung doesn't make a phone like this.
Apple doesn't make one.
Google doesn't make one.

<!-- S56 -->
Instead, you get small companies
making unusual phones
for people who want
something different.
And perhaps that's enough.

<!-- S57 -->
So is the physical keyboard
really dead?
Well...
not quite.

<!-- S58 -->
It's no longer mainstream.
You won't find it in a phone shop.
And you're certainly not going to see
a new BlackBerry on the shelf.

<!-- S59 -->
But the idea didn't disappear.
It became a niche.
And sometimes that's exactly
how technologies survive.

<!-- S60 -->
They don't have to win.
They just have to remain useful
to somebody.

<!-- S61 -->
And that unexpected answer
from the start?

<!-- S62 -->
This script was written
on a phone with a physical keyboard.
This one.

<!-- S63 -->
And so were the scripts
for a lot of other videos
on this channel.

<!-- S64 -->
The physical keyboard didn't really die.
It just became something
you have to look for.

<!-- S65 -->
And if you really want one,
you can still buy one today.
