<!-- S01 -->
Imagine joining a multiplayer game
for the first time.

<!-- S02 -->
In games like World of Warcraft,
Final Fantasy XIV
or The Elder Scrolls Online,
groups are built around
three basic roles.

<!-- S03 -->
You can deal damage.
You can heal your teammates.
Or you can become the tank.

<!-- S04 -->
The person who stands directly
in front of the thing
everyone else is trying very hard
not to stand in front of.

<!-- S05 -->
And of the three,
the tank is often
the one nobody wants to be.

<!-- S06 -->
Which is a little odd.
Because apparently,
this is a very important job.

<!-- S07 -->
The boss is attacking you.
Your teammates are depending on you.

<!-- S08 -->
If you make a mistake,
someone might die.
If you die,
everyone might die.

<!-- S09 -->
And if the group wipes?
Well...
Everyone knows whose fault it is.

<!-- S10 -->
So you might expect tanking
to be incredibly difficult.

<!-- S11 -->
But here's the strange thing.
Sometimes...
it really isn't.

<!-- S12 -->
Let's look at a typical boss fight.
The damage dealers are busy.
They have rotations to maintain.
Abilities to time.

<!-- S13 -->
Resources to manage.
Buffs and debuffs to track.

<!-- S14 -->
They need to stay out of
dangerous areas.
And they need to do all of that
while watching the boss
for the next mechanic.

<!-- S15 -->
The healer has their own problems.
They need to keep everyone alive.
Watch health bars.

<!-- S16 -->
Manage their resources.
And anticipate when
the next big hit is coming.

<!-- S17 -->
And then there's the tank.

<!-- S18 -->
The tank walks towards the boss.
Uses a taunt.
And...
stands there.

<!-- S19 -->
That's it.

<!-- S20 -->
Well, not exactly.

<!-- S21 -->
The tank needs to keep the boss
pointed away from the group.
Block the big attacks.

<!-- S22 -->
Move when necessary.
Keep the boss
where the group wants it.
And occasionally deal with some adds.

<!-- S23 -->
But once everything
is under control...
there can be an astonishing amount
of nothing happening.

<!-- S24 -->
The boss hits you.
You block.
The boss hits you again.
You block again.

<!-- S25 -->
Your abilities come off cooldown.
You press them.
And then...
you wait.

<!-- S26 -->
Meanwhile, your damage dealers
are performing what looks like
a small piano recital
on their keyboards.

<!-- S27 -->
Now imagine one of your damage dealers
makes a mistake.
They stand in something they shouldn't.
They die.

<!-- S28 -->
What does the tank do?

<!-- S29 -->
Usually...
nothing.

<!-- S30 -->
The boss is still attacking the tank.
The healer can resurrect them.
The group can continue.

<!-- S31 -->
In fact, a surprisingly large part
of being a tank
is accepting that other people
are going to make mistakes.

<!-- S32 -->
And that's not necessarily
your problem.
A dead damage dealer is unfortunate.

<!-- S33 -->
A dead tank
is a completely different situation.
Because the boss immediately
finds somebody who cannot take it.

<!-- S34 -->
And that's when things
become interesting.

<!-- S35 -->
The tank isn't really there
to keep everyone alive.
That's the healer's job.

<!-- S36 -->
The tank's job
is to control the encounter.

<!-- S37 -->
The boss should be looking at you.
The boss should be standing
where you want it.

<!-- S38 -->
Its dangerous attacks
should be directed away
from everyone else.

<!-- S39 -->
And when the boss does something
particularly unpleasant...
you should be the one
standing there saying:

<!-- S40 -->
"Whenever you are ready."

<!-- S41 -->
This is why tanking can feel
strangely relaxing.
You're not trying to kill the boss
as quickly as possible.

<!-- S42 -->
You're trying to make the fight
predictable.
And predictable fights
are much easier to survive.

<!-- S43 -->
A role can be extremely important
without requiring you
to constantly do complicated things.

<!-- S44 -->
Imagine a machine with ten components.
Nine of them are doing something
all the time.
One is mostly sitting there.

<!-- S45 -->
But if that one component fails...
the whole machine stops.

<!-- S46 -->
That doesn't mean
the component is complicated.

<!-- S47 -->
It means it's critical.

<!-- S47b -->
By the way, if you'd like to see
where the next question takes us,
consider subscribing.

It really helps the channel.

<!-- S48 -->
Tanking often works exactly like this.
The tank may have fewer things to do
than the damage dealers.

<!-- S49 -->
But some of those things
have enormous consequences.

<!-- S50 -->
Press the wrong button as a DPS?
Maybe you lose some damage.

<!-- S51 -->
Miss a tank mechanic
at the wrong moment?
The boss may kill you.
And now the entire group
has a problem.
Unless there is a second tank.

<!-- S51b -->
And still, hardly anyone
puts their hand up.

<!-- S51c -->
Games have noticed.

<!-- S51d -->
World of Warcraft once added
Call to Arms:
a bag of extra rewards
for whichever role
the queue was short of.

<!-- S51e -->
Final Fantasy XIV has its own version,
and it even has a name.
Adventurer in Need.

<!-- S51f -->
Entire reward systems,
built to bribe somebody
into a role nobody volunteered for.

<!-- S52 -->
So if tanking can be this simple,
why does it feel so intimidating?

<!-- S53 -->
When you're a damage dealer,
you can often make a mistake quietly.
Your damage drops.
Maybe nobody even notices.

<!-- S54 -->
But when you're the tank,
your mistakes are visible.
The boss suddenly turns around.
Someone gets hit.

<!-- S55 -->
The group wipes.
And you immediately know:
"Yeah.
That was me."

<!-- S56 -->
That's enough to make
a perfectly competent player think:
"I don't want to be responsible
for this."

<!-- S57 -->
You don't actually need to be perfect.

<!-- S58 -->
You need to be reliable.

<!-- S59 -->
A tank who does five things
consistently
is worth more than one
who knows twenty tricks
and forgets the basics.

<!-- S60 -->
And there's another reason
tanking is more forgiving
than it looks.
A tank is usually built to survive.

<!-- S61 -->
Lots of health.
High resistances.
Damage mitigation.
Shields.
Blocking.

<!-- S62 -->
The whole character is designed
to be difficult to kill.
Damage dealers are often
the exact opposite.

<!-- S63 -->
Because DDs are usually glass cannons.

<!-- S64 -->
They're built to turn as much
of their survivability as possible
into damage.
And that creates a funny situation.

<!-- S65 -->
A tank can take a bad hit
and lose some health.
A damage dealer takes
the same hit...

<!-- S66 -->
and suddenly they're a corpse.

<!-- S67 -->
So while the tank may have more
responsibility for the encounter,
the tank often has
more room for error.

<!-- S68 -->
Which is probably not
what you'd expect.
The role that looks
the most dangerous...

<!-- S69 -->
may actually be one of
the most forgiving.

<!-- S70 -->
But there is a catch to it.

<!-- S71 -->
Everything I've described so far
works beautifully...
until the encounter
becomes difficult.

<!-- S72 -->
Now the tank may have to position
the boss precisely.

<!-- S73 -->
Move it at specific moments.
Control additional enemies.
Interrupt abilities.
Manage resources.
Swap weapons.

<!-- S74 -->
Swap with the other tank
before the debuffs stack up
and kill you.
Use defensive abilities
before the damage arrives.

<!-- S75 -->
And sometimes perform mechanics
that nobody else in the group can.

<!-- S76 -->
Now the tank isn't just standing there.

<!-- S77 -->
The tank is planning.

<!-- S78 -->
And this is where tanking changes.
You're no longer reacting
to what the boss just did.
You're thinking about
what the boss is going to do next.

<!-- S79 -->
So maybe we can think about tanking
as three different jobs.

<!-- S80 -->
At the first level:
"Don't let the boss
attack everyone else."
That's surprisingly easy.

<!-- S81 -->
At the second:
"Control the battlefield."
Now you're positioning enemies,
managing adds
and helping the group
deal damage safely.

<!-- S82 -->
And at the highest level:
"Understand the entire encounter."

<!-- S83 -->
You know what's coming.
You know where the boss needs to be.
You know when the group
needs your help.

<!-- S84 -->
And you're effectively
conducting the fight.
The first level doesn't have to be
terrifying just because
the third one is difficult.

<!-- S85 -->
You don't learn to drive
by entering a Formula One race.

<!-- S86 -->
And you don't need to tank
the hardest raid in the game
to find out whether you enjoy tanking.

<!-- S87 -->
There is also something
tanking gives you
that damage dealing often doesn't.

<!-- S88 -->
Time.

<!-- S89 -->
More time to look at the encounter.
More time to observe the boss.
More time to understand
what's happening.

<!-- S90 -->
You're not squeezing
every last percent of performance
out of yourself.
You're trying to maintain control.

<!-- S91 -->
And once you've achieved
that control...
you can sometimes just stand there.

<!-- S92 -->
The boss hits you.
You block.
Your teammates do their thing.

<!-- S93 -->
And you think:
"Wait.
Is this really my job?"

<!-- S94 -->
Yes.
That is the job.

<!-- S95 -->
We often assume
that the most important person
in a team
must also be the busiest person.
But that's not necessarily true.

<!-- S96 -->
Sometimes the most valuable thing
you can do is make sure
one very important thing
doesn't go wrong.

<!-- S97 -->
The tank doesn't have to kill the boss.
The tank doesn't have to heal everyone.
The tank doesn't even have to prevent
every mistake.

<!-- S98 -->
The tank simply has to make
the fight manageable.
Predictable.
Controlled.

<!-- S99 -->
And when everything goes well...
it can look like the tank
is doing almost nothing.
Which is perhaps
the ultimate compliment.

<!-- S100 -->
Because when the tank
is doing their job perfectly...
the rest of the group
gets to do theirs.

<!-- S101 -->
And maybe that's why tanking
isn't as scary as it looks.
You aren't carrying the entire group.

<!-- S102 -->
You're just standing in exactly
the place where the boss
wants to kill someone.

<!-- S103 -->
And making sure...
it's you.
